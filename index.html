Training

Drone pilot courses

A staged pathway from your first controlled hover to running an approved operation — built on ICAO Annex 1 principles and mapped to the requirements of the country you'll fly in.

Nepal

CAAN pathway

Delivered from Kathmandu for Nepali operators, cooperatives, agencies and enterprise teams. Course material is written against Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal requirements, including the Personnel Licensing Requirements, and against ICAO Annex 1 principles.

Australia

CASA Part 101 pathway

Delivered from Brisbane for Australian operators and enterprise teams. Course material follows CASA Part 101 practice — remote pilot licensing, category endorsements and the operator certificate that commercial work sits under.

FAQ

Common questions

The things people ask before they pick up the phone. If yours isn't here, ask us directly — we'd rather answer it than have you guess.

Do I need a licence to fly a drone commercially?

In almost every case, yes — and in both Nepal and Australia the requirement applies to the pilot and, separately, to the organisation the work is done under. Flying for payment, for an employer, or as part of a business is treated differently from flying for fun, regardless of the size of the aircraft. We'll walk you through exactly which approvals your intended work needs before you spend money on training you may not require.

What's the difference between a pilot licence and an operator certificate?

A remote pilot licence qualifies you to fly. An operator certificate approves an organisation to conduct commercial operations — it carries responsibilities around procedures, record keeping, safety management and an accountable person. Most commercial work needs both: a licensed pilot, flying under a certificated operator. If you're setting up your own business, you'll eventually need the second one, and we help build it.

How long does training take?

A foundation course runs over a day or two. A full licensing program typically runs across several days of theory plus practical flight time and assessment, and how quickly you get through the practical depends more on your prior stick time than on the calendar. Endorsements added to an existing licence are shorter. We'll give you a realistic schedule for your situation rather than a brochure number.

Do I need my own drone to train?

No. Training aircraft are provided. If you already own the platform you intend to work with, bring it — training on the aircraft you'll actually fly is better, and it lets us cover its specific systems, failure modes and battery behaviour.

I hold a licence from another country. Does it transfer?

Not automatically. Authorities generally require a conversion or validation process rather than straight recognition, and the requirements differ in each direction between Nepal and Australia. Send us what you hold and we'll tell you what the gap actually is — sometimes it's a short conversion, sometimes it's most of a full course.

Can you fly the job for us instead of training our staff?

Yes — that's our operations line. For one-off or occasional work, contracting a crew is almost always cheaper than training, licensing, equipping and insuring your own. We'll tell you honestly which side of that line your workload falls on; if you're flying often enough that an in-house program makes sense, we'd rather build it with you.

Do you sell drones as well as train pilots?

Yes, and we source independently rather than pushing one brand. Tell us the mission — the terrain, the payload, the flight time, the budget — and we'll specify what fits, supply it through the Eastern Aero network, and keep it supported with spares and repair coordination afterwards.

Where do you operate?

Nepal and Australia. Training and operations run out of Kathmandu for Nepal, and out of Brisbane for Australia. For corporate and agency programs we travel to your site in either country.
